SEND

SEND: the pressure point nobody can ignore

Disabled Children's Partnership data shows the scale of family impact. The white paper is coming — here's what to watch and what to do now.

This week, Good Morning Britain highlighted what SEND families have been saying for years: the system pushes parents into crisis mode. Research cited in the segment (Disabled Children's Partnership) found:

  • 37% of SEND parents have quit work
  • 34% have reduced their hours
  • 31% rely on benefits

That's not "hardship around the edges". It's a structural problem showing up as lost income across whole households.

The funding still doesn't add up

EHCPs have risen from around 240,000 (2015) to 638,700 (Jan 2025). Demand has more than doubled, and the system is paying for it in the most expensive way possible.

Independent special school places average about £61,500 per year versus ~£24,000 in state special schools — largely because capacity constraints push councils into premium provision.

High-needs spending has been rising fast and is forecast to keep climbing (IFS forecasts spending above £18bn by 2028–29). The LGA says 8 in 10 councils are at financial risk from SEND costs.

Sources: IFS 2024, DfE SEN2 data, LGA briefing

The SEND deficit "reset" (and why it matters)

Government has said it will cover 90% of historic SEND deficits built up by March 2026. That helps councils' balance sheets — but it doesn't fix the engine causing the deficit in the first place.

IFS is explicit: without reform, councils were on course to build up a further ~£9bn of SEND-related debt across 2026–27 and 2027–28, plus a big in-year funding gap by 2028–29.

The part that doesn't add up

The £200 million training programme covers every teacher in England, plus TAs, FE staff, and early years workers. That's well over a million people. Works out to roughly £200 per person. A two-day specialist SEND course typically costs £300–£500 per delegate. The budget doesn't stretch.

No announcement on improving TA pay — the people who actually deliver most SEND support in mainstream classrooms.

Do this next
  • Review your SEND register and provision map — know where you stand before the white paper.
  • Cost your SEND provision from your own budget (not top-up). You'll need this number.
  • Brief governors with a one-page SEND summary: numbers, costs, gaps.

SEND

SEND transport: the silent budget-wrecker

Costs could rise to £3.4bn/year by 2030/31 — up from ~£2bn recently.

Councils are warning that SEND transport costs could rise to £3.4bn a year by 2030/31 if nothing changes — up from roughly £2bn recently. Longer journeys are the symptom. Lack of local provision is the disease.
